## Key Question 1 — Which Aeroplan Flight Rewards chart is in force in October 2026 (North America ↔ Pacific), when did it change, is "85,000 / 102,500" right, and how are Air Canada's own flights priced?

### Takeaway
Aeroplan changed its Flight Reward Chart effective **June 1, 2026** (announced in spring 2026; coverage is dated from 2026-04-25). For North America ↔ Pacific partner (fixed-price) business class the 7,501–11,000-mile band is now **102,500** one-way (was 87,500) — confirmed by many sources quoting Air Canada's notice. The 5,001–7,500-mile band is **85,000 per Milesopedia and Live and Let's Fly (was 75,000)**, and an August-2026 EVA award alert priced ORD/LAX/SFO/SEA–TPE (all 5,001–7,500-mile routes) at 85,000 — but two trackers (Miles Mosaic, Max Miles Points) still list 75,000, so that cell is **LIKELY 85,000 but UNVERIFIED against the official chart**. No 2025 devaluation was found; the June 2026 change is the one that matters. Air Canada-operated flights are priced dynamically within ranges; observed YYZ–ICN business prices in 2026 were 87,500 (promo-level), 110,000 and 150,000 points one-way.

## Key Question 3 — Fees: partner booking fee, fuel surcharges, taxes, change/cancel fees, stopovers, one-way bookability, booking window

### Takeaway
Partner itineraries carry a CAD 39 per-ticket partner booking fee (payable with points) plus government taxes; Aeroplan does not add carrier-imposed fuel surcharges on partner awards. Phone bookings that could have been made online cost a non-refundable CAD 30. Cancellation is free within 24 hours; afterwards fees apply up to 2 hours before departure (official table quoted: CAD 150 online / 175 by phone to cancel a Standard-type award, CAD 75 online / 100 by phone to change; the exact fare-type mapping for partner business awards is UNVERIFIED). Stopovers cost 5,000 points per passenger per direction (max 45 days); awards are one-way priced; partner space is bookable 355 days out.

### Cited Findings

Aeroplan charges a flat **2,500 points or CAD 25 one-way** for a lap infant on any award, in any cabin, on any partner (free within Canada / Canada–US); infants can be added online, but recent FlyerTalk reports describe the online pricing engine charging a full adult award in error, with the standard workaround being "book the adults online, then call 1-800-361-5373 to add the infant". No source on bassinets or on how the infant document is ticketed was obtained.

## Key Question 6 — Canada transit requirements for connecting at YYZ/YVR

### Takeaway
South Korean passport holders are visa-exempt but need an **eTA (CAD 7, valid up to 5 years) even to transit a Canadian airport without leaving the sterile area**; US citizens do not need one; US lawful permanent residents (green-card holders) of any nationality have been eTA-exempt since April 26, 2022 and must instead carry a valid passport plus a valid green card. Whether a US-origin passenger must physically clear Canadian immigration when connecting to an international flight at YYZ/YVR could not be researched in-session (see Gaps).

### Cited Findings
- "As of April 26, 2022, lawful permanent residents of the United States are also exempt from the eTA requirement and must show these documents for all methods of travel to Canada": a valid passport from the country of nationality and a valid green card (or equivalent proof of US status) — [Government of Canada: eTA eligibility](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/visit-canada/eta/eligibility.html).
- Most visa-exempt travellers "need a visa or an Electronic Travel Authorization (eTA) to fly to, or transit through, a Canadian airport"; a border services officer will ask to see your passport and other documents, e.g. a U.S. Green Card — [Western University IESC eTA page](https://iesc.uwo.ca/visas/electronic_travel_authorization_eta.html).
- Korean-language guidance: the eTA is required for South Korean nationals "even if you don't plan to leave the airport's international transit area"; valid up to five years or until passport expiry; applied online, usually approved within minutes; fee quoted as C$7 (as of Jan 2022) — [koreagapyear.com](https://koreagapyear.com/en/aboutUs/tip/3587).
- 2026 guide: green-card holders flying to Canada must carry a valid green card + passport and generally do not need an eTA — [silaws.com (2026)](https://silaws.com/eta-electronic-travel-authorization-canada-2026-en/); [lawyerinfo.ca](https://lawyerinfo.ca/guides/immigration-visas/can-us-permanent-residents-green-card-holders-fly-to-canada-with-just-an-eta/) (non-official).
- Anecdotal: transit through Canada with a US passport "required nothing beyond a US passport" — [r/canadatravel thread (mirror)](https://twt.omada.cafe/r/canadatravel/comments/1nnebu1/transiting_through_canada_wus_passport) (anecdotal).
- Air Canada's own travel-documents page is the carrier's reference for what will be checked at ORD before boarding a YYZ/YVR flight — [Air Canada travel documents](https://www.aircanada.com/us/en/aco/home/plan/travel-requirements/travel-documents.html) (could not be opened).

### Inferences
- For this party: a Korean-passport adult who is a US green-card holder → no eTA, but must carry the physical green card plus passport; a Korean-passport adult without US PR → eTA (apply at canada.ca, CAD 7, before the ORD departure); a US-citizen adult → US passport only. The infant needs the same document set as its own nationality/status implies (a Korean-passport infant without US PR needs its own eTA — inference from the per-person rule, UNVERIFIED).
- Because the eTA is linked to the passport, a renewed passport needs a new eTA.
